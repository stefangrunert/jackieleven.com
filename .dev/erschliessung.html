1. Starting point

The archive comprises 741 sources about Jackie Leven with original summaries, 73 published columns from 76 recovered texts, twelve research dossiers covering periods of his life, discography, concerts and websites, a calendar of 470 appearances from 1997 to 2011, and a discography with 88 entries. Assembled over a few weeks, it covers his life and work unevenly.

Research agents collected sources in two rounds. In the first, eleven agents worked in parallel, each with a separate search space; a shared allowance of 200 web searches was exhausted after a third of the run, and archive.org was intermittently unavailable. Several areas therefore lack systematic coverage. Searches and unsuccessful leads are recorded in prose in research/quellen/verlorene-spuren.md.

Research has primarily captured documents. Concerts, people, places, broadcasts and life events mentioned in them currently remain within summaries and research prose. They are not yet represented in the ontology. Merely transferring header metadata into the new model would produce many documents but few relationships.

2. Objectives and principles

The aim is a systematically growing collection of entities and evidenced assertions whose creation remains traceable. The following principles apply:

  • Depth before breadth. Convert existing material fully into entities and assertions before adding new sources. These texts have already been read and summarised; extracting their information is inexpensive and quickly enriches the graph.
  • Gaps guide discovery. A gap report generated from the data determines which sources to examine next.
  • Record every search, including unsuccessful ones. Only then can collection completeness be assessed.
  • Agents propose; a person confirms. No agent writes directly to the confirmed dataset.
  • Every assertion records its provenance: who recorded it, under which job, and who reviewed it when.

Existing research rules remain in force. Third-party texts are summarised rather than reproduced in full; Leven's claims are labelled as self-statements; contradictions are identified, not resolved. In the ontology these correspond to evidence type self_statement and competing assertions.

3. Cataloguing existing material

Agents examine existing material document by document and produce ontology-format proposals: entities, evidenced assertions and mentions linked to passages. Different material yields different assertions.

Examining different documents creates duplicate entities: a place or band member may occur in dozens of sources. Before creating an entity, the agent compares its proposal with the existing collection and proposals from parallel jobs, using names, variants and external identifiers where available. Validation flags remaining duplicates.

Processing the concert calendar connects two collections. A concert announced in the calendar and reviewed in a source receives status took_place with the review as evidence. Conversely, reviews without calendar entries establish additional concerts, especially before 1997.

4. Gap report

With every data-repository revision, a tool reports collection distribution. For each year from 1950 onwards, it counts announced and documented concerts, documents by type, language and country, broadcasts and recordings. It also lists assertions with only one reference, disputed assertions, entities without evidence and works without an identified recording. A year with documented touring but no registered concert becomes visible.

The current collection already reveals several gaps:

  • Concerts before 1997. The calendar starts with the earliest archived fan-site calendar. Doll by Doll appearances from roughly 1977–1983 and solo years through 1996 are largely missing.
  • Radio and television. BBC, NRK and German broadcast sessions, interviews and concert recordings are recorded only sporadically.
  • Norway and Germany. Both were important markets. Some Norwegian press requires Norwegian access through the National Library; German regional press is barely represented.
  • Subscription archives. Only metadata is known from Rock's Backpages; the British Newspaper Archive has not been examined.
  • People. Band members, accompanying musicians, producers, promoters and label staff appear in many sources but are not yet entities.
  • Recordings. Which tracks on different releases contain the same recording remains unresolved.
  • Images. Concert and press photographs with documented licences are missing.

Music-press concert listings

British music magazines of the 1970s and 1980s printed weekly concert listings with date, venue and place. Scanned issues can reconstruct much of Doll by Doll's touring. Like website calendars, these entries are announcements and produce concerts with status announced; reviews and news in the same issues provide occurrence evidence. The work is repetitive and extensive but easy to bound: a known number of issues over a known period.

6. Coverage register

The coverage register records which collection was searched and to what extent. Each entry names collection, scope, method, date, researcher, job and result, even when nothing was found.

source: worldradiohistory/melody-maker
scope:
  period: 1977-01/1983-12
  sections: [gig guide, live reviews, news]
method: full_scan
completeness: fully_checked
searched_on: 2026-11-12
searched_by: {agent: claude-code, model: claude-opus-5-5, job: job/2026-11-dbd-gig-guides}
result:
  issues_total: 364
  issues_unavailable: [1979-03-31]
  candidates: 87
notes: The 1979-03-31 issue has not been scanned.

The field completeness distinguishes how much of a collection was actually examined: attempted (attempted), fetched (fetched), readable (readable), fully checked (fully_checked) or aborted (aborted). A full-text search with no results in a scanned year is readable, not fully checked: OCR may miss a name, and its absence from search results does not establish absence from the source.

Negative results prevent repeated fruitless searches. Fully checked collections support statements such as “not listed in Melody Maker concert listings from 1977 to 1983”. The current leads list transfers into the register: documented but unreadable sources become documents with access status “lost”; unsuccessful searches become register entries.

The example numbers are invented; according to the leads list, the 31 March 1979 issue really is missing from worldradiohistory.

7. Jobs, proposals and review

Jobs

Every research task is a job with a fixed search space, stored as a file in the data repository. The current instructions in research/quellen/CODEX-AUFTRAG.md already do this in prose; the new format makes scope, method, expected output and budget machine-readable.

id: job/2026-11-dbd-gig-guides
goal: Doll by Doll concerts from music-press gig listings
scope:
  sources: [worldradiohistory/melody-maker, worldradiohistory/sounds]
  period: 1977-01/1983-12
  classes: [Event, Place]
method: full_scan
output: [candidates, coverage]
budget: {web_searches: 0, page_fetches: 800}
status: open

Execution

Each job runs in its own Claude Code session, worktree and data-repository branch. Sessions use Sonnet; the model is recorded in every job file, and validation rejects jobs using another model. Every session first reads the data repository's job template agents/AUFTRAG.md. A coordinating session creates jobs, starts sessions, tracks status and collects results. Each session appears separately in the desktop app and can be opened, steered or stopped. Within a session, agents may delegate subtasks, such as individual magazine years; results return to that session. Jobs run in parallel when their search spaces do not overlap; the first round already tried this division across eleven search spaces. Agents produce only ontology-format proposals and coverage-register entries. Requests to archive.org and other services are rate-limited.

Usage limits

Agents run through the Claude Code Max subscription without a fixed budget until stopped. The subscription's five-hour window and weekly allowance, shared by all sessions, constrain usage. Paid extra usage is currently disabled; a run reaching its limit pauses until reset without further charges. The coordinator reads usage status before each new job and starts more sessions only while sufficient allowance remains. A job's budget therefore limits scope—page fetches, searches and subtask count—rather than cost.

Review

Proposals arrive as pull requests. CI validates the schema and flags possible duplicates. Review takes place in the diff: individual assertions can be edited or removed before merging. Only a merged assertion counts as confirmed.

Every assertion records who captured it (recorded_by: agent, model and job, or person). Review applies to a particular assertion version: reviewer and date are stored with a content checksum (reviewed_by, reviewed_at, review_hash). Subsequent edits trigger renewed review. A name recorded only at merge time would not establish that every later revision had been reviewed. RDF export maps these fields to PROV-O. Whether low-risk proposals, such as unambiguous GeoNames places, may be approved in batches remains open (section 12).

Parallel jobs

Parallel jobs use separate worktrees and do not see one another's proposals. Two jobs may independently create the same person or venue. Before merging, the coordinator compares new entities with main and other open branches; branches merge sequentially and identified duplicates are consolidated first.

Run traceability

A job records its source-material revision (input_revision), job-template version and model, results, and allowance consumed (usage). This makes a run traceable without access to the model's internal reasoning.

Reference set

Human review alone does not make extraction reliably measurable. A manually catalogued reference set of 20–30 sources of varying difficulty is therefore created. Every template/model version is measured for correctly captured and omitted assertions, incorrectly merged entities, misassigned evidence, correction effort and runtime. The first run's concert reviews provide a suitable basis because their results have already been reviewed.

Spot checks

At regular intervals, a random sample of confirmed assertions is checked again against its evidence. The error rate indicates whether jobs, review or individual agents need improvement.

8. Recording rules

Record metadata, original summaries and short verbatim quotations. Subscription-archive texts are read through lawful access; only metadata, summary and source location enter the dataset, with access marked paywall. Images are included only with a documented licence, recorded per media object.

People are recorded under their published names. Investigative leads remain distinguishable from established findings through their sources, uncertainty and review status. Publication decisions concern the particular material and its relevance to the archive (ontology, section 11).

9. External contributions

The Contribute form receives memories, additions and leads on photographs, tickets or setlists. A contribution becomes a proposal in the data repository: a memory becomes a document of type memory with the contributor as author; its information becomes assertions with evidence type third_party. Publication requires consent covering publication, attribution and CC BY-NC 4.0 licensing. Photographs also require an identified photographer and that person's consent to publication.

Conversations with associates, including band members, promoters and label staff, could fill gaps absent from written sources. They require targeted outreach; whether and when to do this remains open.

10. Metrics

Cataloguing progress is measured through metrics in the gap report:

  • Proportion of years with registered concerts within periods of documented touring;
  • Proportion of announced concerts with occurrence evidence;
  • Proportion of assertions with at least two independent references, counting only established independence and reporting unresolved cases separately;
  • Accuracy, omissions and correction effort against the reference set;
  • Number and age of open proposals;
  • Fully checked collections in the coverage register;
  • Spot-check error rate.

The architecture proposal's test set measures whether growth improves search.

11. Stages

  1. Tools. Formats for jobs, proposals and registers; validation; gap report. This stage requires the ontology schema and data repository.
  2. Existing material. Import the concert calendar; create a research-source sigla index; examine sources, columns and dossiers; transfer the leads list into the register.
  3. Gaps identified by the report. British music-press listings for the Doll by Doll years, BBC Genome, Norwegian and German press, recording research. No fixed order is set. Jobs follow the gap report and available resources and may run in parallel where search spaces do not overlap.
  4. Extended collections. Subscription archives, Contribute submissions and conversations with associates, depending on section 12 decisions.

Stages 1 and 2 coincide with the architecture proposal's second expansion stage, which introduces entities into search.

First run

A first run requires the data repository, a schema for necessary classes, agreed job/register formats and proposal validation. The complete ontology schema is unnecessary. The first job is a pilot with a small verifiable search space. It exercises schema, formats, pull-request review and allowance consumption before larger jobs are assigned.

On 25 October 2026, the first run examined the source collection's 89 concert reviews. It produced 408 entities: 89 documents, 86 events, 88 places, 87 people, 44 publications, 14 groups and one persona. In a sample of twelve events, the coordinator found one error: a missing author. Review also merged a duplicate venue and person, and changed events known only from previews to announced; this produced two new job-template rules. The result was merged into main and then migrated to schema v0.2, giving the 753 assertions stable IDs. The sample was smaller than the planned reference set, so its error rate is only an initial indication.
