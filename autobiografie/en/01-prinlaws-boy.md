## Documented / invented

**Documented (from Leven's own texts):** the railway line from Leslie to Markinch with the train driver who waved, the hawthorn and the scent of May as the “smell of freedom” — Leven's column of May 2003 itself opens with “A beautiful May morning in south Hampshire - Hawthorn flower - May - is out (ne'er cast a clout till May is out)”; Markinch as “home of Haig's whisky”; birth in June 1950 in Kirkcaldy; childhood in Leslie/Prinlaws, a poor working-class district; his father a commercial traveller, later a carpet fitter; his mother from a Geordie family in Blyth, Northumberland; her love of American blues and her ironing to Lightnin' Hopkins when Leven was about twelve; the shrine to the “Black Sophia” in the coal shed of the council house, the family “neither catholic nor protestant”, the playground line “ye MUST be wan urr thi ither” and Drew Johnston's advice “ye dinnae want tae be telling anybody aboot this being here – it'll give them the creepy-crawlies”, both at the age of thirteen; washing cars for “Daredevil” comics; his siblings Ed and Wendy and the tricycle episode with the wooden blocks; the school library in Glenrothes in 1964, “Catch 22” and “The Caine Mutiny” in one week, the view of the Lomond Hills, the Russian women poets and their “silver coated phantom breath”; whisky from the age of eight; the Polish-Ukrainian working men's clubs as the place of his “first real drinking”; Leven's own dating of the start of his performing to 1964; encouragement from Archie Fisher and Hamish Imlach.

**Invented:** the arrangement into one chapter; standing under the bridge; the sentence about the last date in the book; the paragraph on the hardness and tenderness of the neighbourhood; the comparison between commercial traveller and musician; the remark about the underpasses of Glenrothes; the smell of hotel back rooms.

**Not used because undocumented:** an exact date for the deaths of his parents, names of other neighbours, specific addresses.
