## Documented / invented

**Documented (from Leven's own texts unless stated otherwise):** his own dating of the start of his performing to 1964 (“next year will be my fortyth year of playing shows”, November 2003) and the tour name “Forty Years Of Missed Hotel Breakfasts” (March/April 2004); the overcrowded flat in Carlisle at the end of the 60s with the artist Peter Forster and his drawing of the men saluting a dead crow (July 2004); getting the sack at Metal Box in Carlisle over his long hair, with “it COULD happen” and “unlikely but not impossible” (homepage, December 2009); the Christmas post at Carlisle station with the Official Secrets Act and the Blur joke (May 2004); Berlin in 1971 with Steve Club, the Jim Beam stolen while on LSD, the Ascot Lounge, Jesse Ballard and Wayne Grajeda, and the bells on Sunday morning (April 2004); the purchase of the Gallaher guitar in Berlin in 1971 from “Galloping Jim Whitmore”, with the Doc Watson provenance, and its use on every record up to “Creatures of Light and Darkness” (August 2003); the UK tour with the band Man and the departures board at Glasgow Airport with the Hebridean names (August 2009); Joe Kuccer as saxophonist and flautist on “Control” (April 2004); the promotional trip to Madrid for “Control” and the farmhouse in Dorset (September 2004); “Soft Lowland Tongue” as a title on “Control” and Steve Knightley greeting him with a line from it in 2008, a friendship dating from the Exeter years (blog, September 2008).

**Invented:** the arrangement and all transitions; the patterned hotel carpet; the man who finds his armchair taken; the comparison of the saxophonist to a man sending messages out of a burning building; the idea that an instrument is entitled to discretion about where it came from; the men in overalls under the departures board; the scene behind the van.

**Deliberately left open:** why he left Fife. The website biography gives a gang feud; it is not documented.
