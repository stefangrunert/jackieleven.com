## Documented / invented

**Documented (from Leven's own texts):** the farmhouse near Sturminster Marshall in Dorset as the place where “i first found something resembling peace when i emerged from the maelstrom of living dangerously in the North”; the fall from the bicycle in the moonlight with the thought “it's nearly good to be almost alive”; the derivation of the album title “For Peace Comes Dropping Slow” from this Dorset experience and the Yeats poem; Badbury Rings, Wimborne Minster and The Vine at Pamphill (all September 2004); Morts in Poole, the kitchen in which Leven tried to learn songwriting, and the song line about “the man with the furniture van”; Mikki Rain as “wise woman of our tribe in Dorset” and the barge “Queen Phillipa” near Battersea, to which she took him after London concerts (April 2011); Ed in Poole “in his late teens” and the pub The Bricklayers with Hell's Angels and a meat hook (The Past, April 2011); Nick Whiffen, the Poole Pirates speedway and the line “cor, did you see that!” (August 2004); Exeter in the 70s, the Double Locks pub and the friendship with Steve Knightley and Phil Beer (blog, September 2008); Rushcombe Farm with Joe Shaw, the “Rushcombe Ale” laced with strong grass and the cry “Wait till the Rushcombes”; the tour of Spain supporting the Electric Light Orchestra and Leven's phrase “period of psychic festivity” for that summer (September 2004).

**Invented:** the order and all transitions; Morts' never-asked question about how the work was going; the sentence about learning the names; Ed's expression in the Bricklayers; the description of how a sentence changes direction under Rushcombe Ale; the closing paragraph on the reversed order of peace and destruction.

**Marked as legend:** The ELO tour rests entirely on Leven's own account, without any documentation, and appears here as he told it.
