## Documented / invented

**Documented (from Leven's own texts):** Bristol Gardens as an entire squatted block together with Formosa Street and Clifton Villas, the decaying ochre houses with oriental-style window arches and the taxi drivers who received the address “with careful hostility”; Leven's own list of the residents (from “violent Scottish gangs, prostitutes, heroin addicts, daft western Sufis” to the “desperate young rock bands”); that he and Doll by Doll lived there “in the seventies and eighties”; the pub The George with the Canadian landlady Mrs Bonner, who never gave credit and whose voice Leven thought had been trained in Brooklyn bars; the postcards from every Doll by Doll tour, which she kept; the almost daily round of police intimidation; the pub The Shirland, the five pints of Guinness at lunchtime and the rehearsing and writing afterwards; his parents' visit, their capacity for drink, his mother's friendship with the landlady, the neighbour waiting outside the café with a knife, and his mother's line “Yer livin' in a street full o buggers” in broad Geordie (The Past, September and December 2008); his arranging the funerals of both parents; Fairnington near Kelso “thirty years ago” with his second wife Gillian, his father-in-law Bill as a horse dealer with a Range Rover, the Saturday mornings in the hotel bar on the Tweed, Henry Douglas-Home as the brother of the former prime minister, and the stone with the woodcock that he had painted himself, thrown on LSD at Newcastle station a week later (blog, December 2008).

**Invented:** the order and all transitions; the image of the parents as cats on a radiator; the prepared conversation and the tidying up; the father's verdict on the neighbour; the observation about the identical manners of the very top and the very bottom; the sober reinterpretation of the stone-throwing anecdote; the paragraph about “wild”; the closing paragraph with the Tuesday in 1979.

**Caution:** For the second marriage there is only this one statement by Leven himself — no surname, no date, no independent source. Leven calls the hotel in Kelso “Klenham House”; it is very probably the Ednam House Hotel.
