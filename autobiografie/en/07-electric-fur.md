## Documented / invented

**Documented:** the attack “just off the Caledonian Road in North London” on the way home to Maida Vale, in Leven's own words “I was walking home to Maida Vale and I just got mugged, basically” (interview, “The Big Untidy” no. 7, Berlin 1995); the anabolic steroids for the larynx and the sensation “a feeling like you were covered in electric fur”, together with massive weight gain (ibid. and Telegraph 2000); the contradictory accounts of how long he was without a voice (Leven 1995 “about two years”, Telegraph 2000 “nearly 18 months”, Independent 2001 “several years”); the solo deal with Charisma/Virgin, during whose recordings the attack happened; Leven's verdict on heroin: “I actually think in it's own way it can be a very, very useful drug” (1995); Little Venice, the Warwick Castle pub and the “Jeff” scene, after which he moved away (column, January 2004); his self-description “at this time, i was a dangerously unstable person” (ibid.); what set off his withdrawal — children by the canal in Little Venice listening to a jazz band (Cooking Vinyl press release, 2003) — and “symptom-free within five weeks” (Telegraph 2000); the loss of the falsetto and Leven's statement that for a year he dreamt every morning that it had come back (ibid.).

**Invented:** the reasoning behind the refusal — the story as a second injury, the difference between “not spoken” and “not for sale”; the four hours and the arithmetic nobody can be made to follow; the comparison with the tourniquet; the detail of the towpath scene (only the occasion is documented); the closing reflection on the changed voice.

**Caution:** Leven did not write about the attack itself even once in his 76 texts for his own website, nor about Diana. The chapter makes this gap its subject instead of filling it with invented detail. The year remains open (1983 or 1984) because the sources contradict each other. Two quotes, “a feeling like you were covered in electric fur” and “I actually think in it's own way it can be a very, very useful drug”, come from the interview in The Big Untidy (1995) and the Telegraph 2000; their wording is not secured in the local corpus.
