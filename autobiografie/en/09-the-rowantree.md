## Documented / invented

**Documented:** that Oban was not a place of residence but a destination he returned to again and again — “I was living in London, so first I had to fly to Glasgow” — first with friends at Christmas, later alone, staying at the Rowantree Hotel; the small ferry to Kerrera with the signal board you turn round to call the ferryman, and Gylen Castle, both in the song “Gylen Gylen”; that Leven had not written any songs since the Doll by Doll days, could not judge their quality and played them to no one; the writing of “The Crazy Song”, “Gylen Gylen” and “Heartsick Land” in a single afternoon; the “killer instinct” as a writing discipline, with the sentence “no matter what else is happening in your life, be assured that this writing must be done”; the formula taken over from CORE committee meetings, “yes, that's the principle, but what's the process?”; “I'd gone off drugs and violence for a while” and the evening-long drinking of whisky with lager in Glasgow (all: *The Past Didn't Go Anywhere*, August 2009); the happiest moment, “sitting alone on the beach at Ganavan sands near Oban watching the ferry steam over” (homepage, October 2009).

**Invented:** the thought on the difference between moving somewhere once and going back again and again; the radiator and “the first afternoons of that kind i had had since about 1976”; the image of the aired house; the closed shutters; the impossibility of telling fear from discipline; the wet Tuesday; the closing interpretation of the Ganavan image.

**Caution:** The nine-year break from writing is a calculation, not a figure given by Leven — he names no year. The years of the Oban stays are likewise entirely absent from his texts.
