## Documented / invented

**Documented:** the father's illness — “He took a year to die from when he was diagnosed”, his having been brushed off as a hypochondriac beforehand, the old Cortina over the Falkland Hill to the auctions in Ladybank (Triste interview, October 2001); the quirk about duck, with “maybe it's just a personal thing” (columns, March 2003 and February 2004); the father's passport photograph and the comparison with Burt Lancaster in “Valdez Is Coming”, with “There can be no greater compliment” (October 2003); “when my father died, i hurt myself by working too hard for too long” (November 2004); the compulsive looking for his father in bars; the gift of the Minoan double axe on 18 June 2004 (“Jockstock”) and the fig tree sent by post by Peter Forster, the friend from Carlisle, and the shrine that grew out of them (July 2004); the mother in sheltered housing and later in a nursing home near Leven, her statement that she was being “literally bored to death” (May and October 2003); the afternoons together in the Osborne View pub looking out over the Solent, her childhood in Blyth with the Dutch sailors in wooden clogs, and the parents' courtship on the River Wey (November 2004, September 2004); that she could not drive and kept the car “just so she could sit in it alone, remembering” — the basis of “Woman In A Car” (August 2003); the man in Bexhill who took money off her, and “guess i'll write some songs instead” (May 2003); her death at the end of October/beginning of November 2004 and “I sang these words today at the funeral of my mother who died some days ago” on “Bobby Shaftoe”, which also closes “A Little Voice In Space”; the wedding ring from the care staff, clearing out her room as a “pitiful task” and the postcards from his tours that she had kept; “Her long slow decline wrapped itself round me like an anchor”; the cancellation of the Christmas concert in Milford with explicit reference to the mistake made after his father's death; the two concerts in Münster, “for me it was most healing”; the burial on the coast west of Portsmouth; “an overweight middle-aged Scottish dreamer whose mother always insisted had the sexual dash of Rudolf Valentino” (all: column, November 2004).

**Invented:** the reading of the word “hypochondriac” as a class judgement; the image of the Isle of Wight as a whale; “i sometimes think i was there”; the remark about his hands when introducing the song; “kept the way you keep something” and the comparison with a letter from the front; the distinction between work as a hiding place and work as a hand on your back; “i have never yet found a way of putting that down which does not shame me”; the closing line.

**Caution:** The place of the mother's death is contradictory in Leven's texts — in 2003/04 consistently south Hampshire (nursing home, Hill Head), in 2007 a memory of Bexhill-on-Sea “towards the end of her life”. The chapter follows the contemporaneous version. The date of the father's death (June 2000) comes from the research compendium, not from Leven's own texts.
