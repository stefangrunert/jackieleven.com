## Documented / invented

**Documented:** Crossing Border in The Hague on 14 November 2003 with Ian Rankin, Ciaran Carson, David Thomas and Michael Cosgrave — sold out immediately, no rehearsal, “none of these guys know each other at all”; the DVD that came out of it, “The Meeting Of Remarkable Men” (column, November 2003, July 2004); the premiere of “Jackie Leven Said” on Tuesday 27 January 2004 in the Strathclyde Suite of the Royal Concert Hall at Celtic Connections, Rankin's reading in six roles, the songs Leven and Cosgrave slotted in, and the unscripted banter “as only 'twa twisted Fifers' can” (February 2004); the album about “the lives and childhoods of me, Ian and John Rebus, who is also from Fife” and Leven's remark that the persona had changed and that this was liberating (August 2004); Ubudoll with David Thomas and “our fifth date in 5 years” (February 2004), and Thomas's recitation of “Faces” (April 2003); James Hillman as “my friend the American thinker” and his sentence about the black snake (March 2003); Richard Olivier and “making 100 men cry for a week in the middle of nowhere with Robert Bly” (July 2003); the Findhorn weekend after his father's death at which Bly made the participants look into Leven's face (September 2007); Liam Carson, his mailings of Irish-language books, the IMRAM festival and the bilingual evening at the Crawdaddy (Old Harcourt Station) in Dublin on 25 September 2009 with Cosgrave and the Gaelic Jazz Project and poems by Akhmatova, Mayakovsky and Tsvetaeva; Carson's autobiography “Call Mother A Lonely Field”, named after Leven's song (*Be My Friend*, December 2009); the song “Here Come The Urban Ravens” instead of a Coyne cover for a tribute album, the Nuremberg concert in April 2006 with the warm applause at the name of Coyne, and the conversation with Helmi Coyne; the double bill with Johnny Dowd in Kufstein, home-made absinthe included (column, May 2006); the work on the Willard Grant Conspiracy production by Malcolm Lindsay in summer 2007 with “Russian-style choral work and guitar orchestras” and “38th member - i'm proud” (September 2007); “as long as you accept and remember that there is danger, you're safe” on working with Bly and Thomas (ibid.).

**Invented:** the tracing of the four roads into the hall in The Hague; “for once a title was not a lie”; the comparison of Thomas's effect with weather on a house; “i have been the exhibit before, but rarely so accurately”; “mostly it turns out to be the person they are married to”; the remark about novelists and the age of fifty-four; “the nearest i came”; the closing sentence about how to keep going; the entire closing thought on the lone voice as a marketing category and the fingerprints on the glass.

**Caution:** Whether Leven himself had a leading role at the Findhorn weekends or was a participant is not clear from his texts; the chapter leaves it open. He does not name the title of the Willard Grant album.
