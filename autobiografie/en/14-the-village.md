> From here on the voice changes: in his texts from 2008 onwards Leven writes a capital “I”, uses fewer dashes and more brackets. This chapter follows that later style.

## Documented / invented

**Documented:** the opening with the market hall clock at 6.30 a.m. and the three-mile walk to muck out, “a blunt stab of sonic info for commoners who may need to know that it's TIME”; Stacy's 40th birthday in the market hall with a buffet, a rockabilly band and the unspoken negotiation of a place at the tables; the route across three fields, the Roma camp with the trotting ponies and the wordless nod, the graffiti tunnel under the London–Portsmouth railway line, the centuries-old barns and the hidden blue and yellow steam engine, with Leven's remark that he first saw it under light snow and that nobody could explain it; the horses Smartie (“retired racehorse”) and Blue (“big French horse with a sense of humour”), the walk there four times a day and breaking the ice on the water trough in winter (all: blog, captured September 2011, written spring 2011); the tomcat Mr Big, who head-butts him while he works, the terrier Basil with the lime from the Co-op (there were no lemons), later Ronnie, and the very old cat (homepage, 2008 and 2011); the orchard with vines around the old donkey shed, damsons and apples; the tidal creek in which the terriers retrieve driftwood; Kevin, the “village alco-mystic”, and the conversation about the Ford factory in Southampton; the Sunday jazz session in the pub opposite, with the farmers in petrol-station shirts and “harvest-coloured eyes”; the death of his brother Ed, Ed's time in Poole “in his late teens”, the summer and winter together in Dorset with walks to cider pubs; the tricycle episode with the sister and the wooden blocks, with “my debut as a therapist had not really gone that well”; “Snowdrops will always make me think of Ed now” by the River Hamble; the chorus written at the Hotel Kolpinghaus in Münster (“the rooms are like cells in a monastery”), which only became a song after Ed's death — “Beware Soul Brother” — and its temporary use as the album title; “I'm feeling a bit less roughed up than previously” together with thanks for the sympathy; “Air Crash Investigation” as an outlet for grief and the statement about the “as yet unfinished grief about the loss of my parents, brother and loved ones”; “a sort of Belfast Of The Mind in which the old conflicts remain raw in the imagination”; Jung's sentence “the gods have become diseases” and Leven's own alternative; writing in the garden at night by moonlight, Anne Ridler's “the light of the moon is visible silence” and the blossom petals together with the taped-up Dyson.

**Invented:** the flashback to the 1979 squat as a contrast; the sentence about the “haunted Celtic bar-room prophet” with the wheelbarrow; the line-up of the gods as “two horses, a market hall clock, a steam engine in a hedge, and an old man with ponies”.

**Caution:** The year of his brother's death is unresolved — Leven's own texts point to early 2011, Paul Du Noyer to February 2010. The chapter follows Leven's dating, because it is his voice. The place name Botley is given by him only elsewhere and is deliberately left out here.
