## Documented / invented

**Documented:** the chord sequence E–A–B–E–A–B, called “Summer's End” by Joe Shaw, with Leven's remark that he probably mentions it every year, and the “great sad golden chord”; the farmer's removal of the stile by the railway line, the only place to sit on that walk, and Leven's self-criticism about the “paralysing sense of complaint” and the turning of “generative ritual” into “sentimental ceremony”; “If everything's sacred, then nothing is sacred”; “it is, after all, as important to conceal as to reveal. And nature loves to hide” and “Our culture is addicted to revelation - it's pathetic”; “what is revealed is always in a frame” with the image of the infant touching the frame; “a writer needs cold places to which to return alone” and the notebooks with lines that never become songs; “as long as you accept and remember that there is danger, you're safe”; the four legends (the whisky “Leven's Lament”, Shannon Doyle, the lighthouse with the dog biscuits, the Romany gathering in Lebanon); the ferry scene with the signal board turned round for Kerrera; the mother ironing to Lightnin' Hopkins and the shrine of the “Black Sophia” in the coal shed; the mugging in north London and the altered voice; the three Oban songs written in one afternoon; the figure “over four hundred songs” (from the website operator's obituary text); Michael Cosgrave, Joe Shaw, Deborah Greenwood, the fan club with its PO box in Derbyshire, the horses, the terriers, the market hall clock, the tidal creek with kingfishers.

**Invented:** the arrangement as a legacy; the entire section on the story as a form of hospitality; “the number would be a full stop”; the tally of his luck in “the usual units”; the scene with the falling apples; the interpretation of the chord at the end; the three closing sentences.

**Context:** This chapter is the freest invention in the book. Leven never wrote a summing-up of his life — his last blog entry ends with the complete text of his song “Working Alone” from “Forbidden Songs Of The Dying West”, a ploughman's image of time passing and of the reins passing to another. That would be the real ending. It does not appear here because the song text is protected by copyright and should not be reproduced without permission.
