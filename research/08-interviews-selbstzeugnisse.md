# Jackie Leven — Interviews, Porträts und Selbstzeugnisse

Recherche-Stand: 17. September 2026. Diese Datei sammelt längere Interviews, Porträtartikel und Selbstzeugnisse, in denen Jackie Leven (geboren als Alan Moffatt, 18. Juni 1950 in Kirkcaldy, Fife; gestorben 14. November 2011 in Hampshire) sein Leben selbst erzählt. Die Zitate sind im englischen Original wiedergegeben und jeweils mit Quelle und Entstehungskontext versehen. Die Anordnung folgt chronologisch den behandelten Lebensthemen. Wichtige Primärquellen sind dabei die offizielle, von Leven autorisierte (mutmaßlich weitgehend selbst verfasste) Website-Biografie [6], sein eigener Blog [7], die ZigZag-Interviews von Kris Needs aus den Jahren 1978–1981 [1] und die letzten großen Interviews von 2008–2011 [12][13][15].

---

## Kindheit und Jugend in Fife (1950 bis ca. 1967)

Leven beschrieb sich durchgehend als Außenseiter von Geburt an. In einem Herald-Porträt (ca. 2006, wiedergegeben im theartsdesk-Nachruf) sagte der 56-Jährige: „I was always an outsider in Scotland. My family had a lot of Romany content and back in the 1950s in Fife, before there was even a road bridge, that really made us outsiders. In my teens, I couldn't even go home at night because gangs would be waiting for me. I remember one night I was hiding under some old boards in an allotment all night in the pissing rain, while these bastards were hanging around. In the end I'd been there so long I had to go straight to work, where I got a bollocking for being a terrible mess. And I thought: I can't carry on like this.“ [16]

Im Guardian-Interview von 2001 erzählte er dieselbe Vertreibungsgeschichte knapper: „I was picked on by a complete bastard who had a small gang. I couldn't get into my own house for weeks.“ Daraufhin habe er als Landarbeiter von Cornwall bis Glasgow gearbeitet. [3]

Die offizielle Website-Biografie (Stand 2008) beschreibt die Familie: Vater „Irish Cockney“, Mutter aus einer großen northumbrischen (Geordie-)Familie, beide Romany-Hintergrund; die Mutter habe amerikanischen Blues geliebt („Lightnin' Hopkins“), Leven habe in Kirkcaldy Folk-Clubs wie dem „Elbow Room“ gespielt und sei dort von Archie Fisher und Hamish Imlach ermutigt worden; die Gang-Fehhde habe ihn schließlich aus Fife und Schottland vertrieben. [6]

Ein eindrückliches Selbstzeugnis der religiös-kulturellen Fremdheit liefert Levens eigener Blog (Juni 2008): Seine Mutter habe im Kohlenschuppen des Council House einen Schrein für die „Black Sophia“ gehabt — „a large painted wooden statue of a black woman“. Die Familie sei „neither catholic nor protestant“ gewesen, was im Schottland der 1950er/60er Erklärungsnot auslöste: „'ye MUST be wan urr thi ither' was a much-heard menacing refrain at school.“ Er erzählt, wie der orangene Pfadfinder-Freund Drew Johnston den Schrein entdeckte und ihm riet: „ye dinnae want tae be telling anybody aboot this being here – it'll give them the creepy-crawlies“. [7]

Zur Schulzeit: Leven besuchte die Kirkcaldy High School ein Jahr unter Gordon Brown. Im März 2010 sagte er über den damaligen Premierminister: „I'm a fan of Gordon's, a big supporter. What you see is what you get and you have to respect that.“ [10] Mehrere Quellen berichten, er sei der erste schottische Schüler gewesen, der wegen Drogen von der Schule verwiesen wurde [18][19][20]. Paul Du Noyer (The Word, März 2012) fasst zusammen: Leven sei als Alan Moffatt geboren worden, habe in der Schulbibliothek „every poet from John Clare to the early 20th century Russians“ verschlungen, sei zum Schreiben auf die Hügel Fifes geschwänzt und habe sich das Singen durch Nachahmen von Fontella Bass' „Rescue Me“ (1965) beigebracht. [20]

In dem (hinter einer Bezahlschranke liegenden) Interview, das Alastair McKay im März 1994 führte, beginnt Leven mit der Ortsangabe: „I was born in Kirkcaldy, but I suppose I'm really more of a Glenrothes person.“ [2] (Nur der Anfang ist frei zugänglich; siehe „Offene Fragen“.)

## Wanderjahre: South Bank, Kerry, Berlin, Madrid — „John St Field“ (Ende der 1960er bis 1973)

Die offizielle Biografie beschreibt Jahre des Umherwanderns: vier Monate als obdachloser Straßenmusiker am South Bank Centre in London, Aufenthalte in der Grafschaft Kerry (Irland), in Berlin und Madrid, wo 1971 unter dem Namen John St Field das Album „Control“ erschien; danach Leben in besetzten Häusern, wo er Menschen mit schweren psychischen Störungen kennenlernte — Leven zitiere dabei gern Theodore Roethke: „for what is madness but nobility of soul at odds with circumstance?“ [6]

Der Independent-Nachruf (Nick Hasted) ergänzt: Arbeit als Hilfsarbeiter in Kerry, Berlin und Madrid; zeitweise obdachlos am South Bank; eine erste Ehe mit 16; Hasted vermerkt auch die (sonst nirgends belegte) Bemerkung, Leven habe einmal erwähnt, eine Millionärin geheiratet zu haben. [18]

Zum Pseudonym John St Field sagte Leven laut Du Noyer: „I was in a little trouble with the forces of law and order.“ [20]

## Doll by Doll (ca. 1975–1982/83)

### Selbstdeutung in den ZigZag-Interviews 1978–1981 (Kris Needs)

Die frühesten erhaltenen Lang-Interviews stammen von Kris Needs (ZigZag). Im Juni 1978, nach einer Prügelattacke auf die Band in Silvertown, sagte Leven über die Wirkung der Konzerte: „One thing that interests us, to say the least, is we have had a few people who, as a direct result of coming to our gigs, have ended up in asylums. They send us postcards and letters regularly.“ Und: „I think it's very much that we don't compromise and we have a show which has a real sense of dynamics, and if you're at a vulnerable point in your life and you take the whole show in, then there's no doubt it'll have the effect it's designed to have and that it's going to turn you right round to have a look at yourself. If you don't like what you see in the mirror then it's not our fault.“ [1]

Zum Verhältnis zum Publikum: „We want people to face the reality of their own life. I think the crux of it that we reduce people to individuals rather than encourage a sense of false comradeship … It's you and me as far as I'm concerned. Not US against THEM because I don't believe there is a THEM.“ [1]

Die Band spendete damals Gage an R. D. Laings Philadelphia Association; Leven beschrieb sich — aus seinem „half-Romany background“ heraus — als „necromancer“, der „the future through communication with the dead“ offenbare. [1]

Anfang 1979 (vor dem Debütalbum „Remember“) berichtete Leven einen Suizidversuch kurz vor Weihnachten 1978: „I had a suicide attempt just before Christmas. If Jo and Joni hadn't got me to the hospital ultra-quick it would have been all over. I took a fatal overdose of reds, tenuate, valium and booze. I was enjoying myself. I knew what I was doing. I was really unhappy about things.“ Dazu die düstere Bandprognose: „This band is unquestionably involved in a death trip, and when you're involved in a death trip it ends in one thing alone and that's death.“ [1]

1980/81, vor dem dritten Album, der erfolgsorientierte Ton: „With Remember and Gypsy Blood we tied up the loose ends for ourselves … Now I think we can award ourselves a larger audience … We've looked to old albums by the Velvet Underground and the Doors, not lifted ideas but lifted the spirit in which they were recorded and grafted it onto what we've been doing. I like to think that we're part of that tradition.“ Und 1981: „You just can't go round doing the artistic equivalent of being a terrorist … You can't do it and expect to get away with it.“ [1]

### Rückblick 2007 (Wiederveröffentlichungen, eigene Linernotes und Interviews)

In seinen Notizen zur „Remember“-Wiederauflage (2007) schrieb Leven über den Punk-Kontext: „We were intimidating, punk was like going to a funfair where some of the rides were scary but you knew in advance you were going to be scared. … We did like the fact that people were both exhilarated and shell-shocked.“ Und selbstkritisch: „I don't think I was particularly well mentally. I'm prepared to be quite candid about that now. I took far too many drugs … it made me violent, it made me unpredictable, it made me dangerous. I was a real mess. I had seriously bad things on my mind and I really made the band suffer. I could have done all that better. But I think the band would agree, we probably wouldn't have been Doll By Doll if that been the case.“ [1]

Bei Neil McCormick (Telegraph, 12. April 2007) deutete Leven die Band psychologisch: „Jung said, 'Love is matching psychic disturbance', and I often think that's what we had in Doll By Doll. We were all refugees from big traumas, finding a place, possibly for the first time in our lives, where we steadied into a belief that we could in some way rescue our eternal souls through art.“ Zum Scheitern: „It was certainly difficult to see how we were going to make a better record than Gypsy Blood. I remember having the disturbing sense that even if we did, it would make no difference, because something else was wrong and we didn't know what that was, other than the fact that we were architects of wrongness. We cast ourselves in a set of images which genuinely closed doors for us, like shut the whole castle up, with us standing around outside pissing against the parapets going 'Come on, ye bastards!'“ Zur Niederlage: „The bitterness of the defeat was a true bitterness. But it's that salting of wounds that, frankly, we all need in our life.“ [11]

Im Herald-Porträt (ca. 2006) bekannte er: „I think I was genuinely mentally ill. Not all the time, but episodically, when my drug-taking – which wasn't narcotic at that time, more LSD – really got a grip of me.“ Und über die Bandzeit insgesamt: a „self-built ritual and initiation that got me out of boyhood and into manhood, but it was a very, very heavy way to go.“ [16]

Nick Hasted (Independent) zitiert Leven aus einem Gespräch von 2007 über „Gypsy Blood“ (von ihm „Gypsy Heart“ geschrieben, sic): „I've never had my own place to live, I've always ended up living at other people's, and that continues to this day. But that record made me feel I had a place in the world, even if it was a very unpleasant place that wasn't going to do me any favours.“ [18]

Zur legendären Devo-Tour, von der die Band flog, schrieb Leven: „One of the funniest things that ever happened to us was being thrown off the Devo tour. They hated us, beyond endurance. Once we got over being stunned … we sat in our hotel in New Castle and tried to trace where it started. [Guitarist] Jo [Shaw] decided it was because he went and asked them if they had any beer …“ [14]

## Der Überfall 1983/84 und die Heroin-Jahre

Die Darstellungen variieren in Details (siehe „Widersprüche“). Guardian 2001: Nach einer Aufnahmesession wurde er in der Market Street in Islington angegriffen und mit einem Schnitt über die Kehle verletzt; monatelang konnte er nicht sprechen, sein Falsett war für immer verloren; er wurde heroinabhängig. [3]

Im Herald-Porträt datierte er den Überfall auf 1984: nach einer Flasche Canadian Club und einer aus dem Studiofenster geworfenen Schreibmaschine sei er auf dem Heimweg von Nord-London nach Maida Vale attackiert worden: „I got properly mugged. I probably would have been killed, except the guys I was working with decided to come looking for me and probably saved my life.“ Zwei Jahre konnte er nicht sprechen oder singen. Über die Heroinzeit: „When you can't talk, people begin to treat you like you're mentally ill. So I began to feel mentally ill, and in the end I didn't go out of the house at all. In that state of withdrawal, I think I used heroin in a proper and perhaps appropriate way to deal with a lot of pain. But I never liked it as a lifestyle. It was a load of shit.“ [16]

Dem Guardian sagte er später über die Sucht: „I was spending £150 a day, and found I had no money.“ [17] Die offizielle Biografie spricht von einem „unprovoked street attack“, bei dem er „nearly murdered by strangulation“ wurde; er habe Plattenvertrag, Freunde und Orientierung verloren und fast ein Jahr isoliert mit Heroin gelebt. [6] Der Scotsman (Somerville, der Leven seit 1994 kannte) nennt die Variante: Drosselung von hinten an einem Geldautomaten in Islington. [19]

## Genesung, CORE Trust und Prinzessin Diana

Über die Genesung ab 1985: Mischung aus chinesischer Akupunktur — „a really powerful tool against addiction“ — und „psychic healing“; auf Anregung seines Arztes gründete er mit Carol Wolf den CORE Trust, ein holistisches Suchthilfezentrum in London. [16] Die Biografie bestätigt: Wiedereintritt „in die Welt“ 1985 nach „traditional Chinese five-element acupuncture and psychic healing“. [6] 2001 sagte er über seine heutige Substanz-Empfindlichkeit: „If I take an aspirin these days I'm smashed.“ [3]

Über Diana, Princess of Wales (Schirmherrin von CORE), erzählte er Neil McCormick 2007: Sie sei „very funny, warm, engaged, genuinely charming“ gewesen. „The first time we had lunch together, she leaned into me and said, 'What are you doing after?' in this quiet, sexy voice. I said, 'Beg your pardon, ma'am?' She just laughed: 'Only kidding!'“ Diana habe ihn backstage bedrängt, für sie zu singen — „a moment that instilled in him the notion that he might perform again. 'She did a wonderful thing for me, for which I will always be grateful.'“ [11] Die Website-Biografie gibt die Szene so wieder: „I understand you used to be a singer“, sagte sie. „'I AM a singer' was his bristling reply. 'Well, sing something now' she suggested.“ Er sang die schottische Weise „The Bonnie Earl of Moray“, die Grundlage von „Main Travelled Roads“. [6] McCormick schrieb 2011 im Nachruf, Leven habe stets Diana zugeschrieben, ihn zur Rückkehr zur Musik bewogen zu haben. [21]

Zu einer weiteren Lebenswendung dieser Jahre: Seine langjährige Partnerin und CORE-Mitgründerin lief mit dem Leibwächter und Akupunkteur des Dalai Lama davon — Levens Kommentar 2001: „It fizzled out shortly afterwards, to my great sense of well-being.“ [3] (Du Noyer ergänzt, der Leibwächter habe Leven zum „Wiedergutmachung“ eine Kassette des Dichters Robert Bly gegeben — der Beginn von Levens Bly-Phase. [20])

## Rückkehr zur Musik (Oban, ab 1994) und Selbstverständnis als Songwriter

Nach der CORE-Zeit zog Leven nach Oban an der Westküste Schottlands: „He spent the nights in bars with fishermen and forester friends, and the days writing the songs“ — die Basis des Comeback-Albums „The Mystery Of Love Is Greater Than The Mystery Of Death“ (1994). [6] Der Independent spricht von der „fierce warmth“ der Trawler-Männer in den Bars. [18]

Über die Quelle seines Schreibens sagte er im Herald-Porträt: „I sometimes think my problem is that I'm too connected to the pain of other people – it really breaks me up. Out of that, and anger about that, I find myself writing. I'm not entirely sure that's a healthy way to do things, but I think that's where it comes from.“ [16]

Zur Produktivitäts-Ökonomie am Rand des Geschäfts sagte er 2007: „The economics of a life in the margins means I've got to make a lot of records and sell them to the few people who are interested. It makes you drop a load of bullshit about being an artist, keeps you working hard and honestly. I have acquired rigour.“ [11] Und zum historischen Rang von Doll by Doll: „It's a bit like what Chairman Mao said when asked if the French Revolution was a success or not. … It's too early to tell.“ [11]

Im Doppel-Interview mit Ian Rankin (Paul Du Noyer, The Word, April 2004) erläuterte er seine keltische Weltdeutung: „There is a Celtic idea that reality lies betwixt and between. So the space between the tree and the bark of the tree is the all-important space, and those spaces exist within us as personalities.“ Zur emotionalen Sprachlosigkeit der Ostküsten-Schotten: „Reticent to the point that, when it bursts forth, it's mighty in its joy and its need to conjoin. So we overdo it. That's what the pubs are about. I really like that culture: it's full of people having a holiday from that reticence.“ [5] Zu dieser Zeit spielte er etwa 130 Shows pro Jahr („It used to be 200 but they're better paid now“) und berichtete, seine CDs lägen sogar in Shanghai in den Läden. [5]

Zum Pseudonym Sir Vincent Lone sagte er 2010 im Chicago Reader: „My label, Cooking Vinyl, said that the dynamic laws of market phasing require 18 months to properly market a Jackie album. But the songs are too good to waste, so I just hand them off to Vince, who unlike me can record an album in three or four days.“ [14] 2008 bei Vanguard Online: „Vince has acquired a lot of personality. And also he's selling very well! … Vince albums are diffusion Jackie albums, where they don't cost as much to make. … I like the Vince albums, especially the new one, Troubadour Heart … as much as the Jackie albums.“ [13]

Über die Genreschubladen: „What the fuck is folk-rock about anything I've ever done?“ — und „Celtic soul“ sei „better than nothing but probably doesn't do me any favors, given that I don't sound anything like Van Morrison.“ [14]

### Letzte große Interviews: Vanguard Online (2008/09) und Pennyblackmusic (2011)

Im August 2008 (E-Mail-Interview zu „Lovers At The Gun Club“) erklärte er sein Album-Prinzip: „I find a bunch of songs that seem like a 'family' of sorts, although I don't really understand where the blood ties between the songs are. Also, I always have a fairly old song and a fairly new one on each record.“ [12] Zur Rolle des Alkohols in seinem Werk gab er eine programmatische Antwort: „We live in a culture which has abandoned formal initiation of young men and women into the mysteries of the adult world … in such initiations there may well be a place for consciousness changing agents, of which alcohol could be one. Instead we have teenagers honestly attempting to initiate themselves in high street bars every Saturday night, and a hideous scene it is too. … A large component of my writing comes from observing the fallout from this process. If I lived in a Muslim country I would probably write lines like – 'and I see the young men, sitting in cafes, sober as fuck'...“ [12] Auf die Frage, wofür er in Erinnerung bleiben wolle: „It would be heartwarming to be rediscovered in the future for GYPSY BLOOD by Doll By Doll.“ [12]

Im Dezember 2008 (Pub-Interview in Leeds) sagte er zum Schreiben: „I can never understand people that talk about the difficulties of writing. I seem to have acquired a clear idea, right the way down to when I die, of all the stuff I'd like to write.“ Zu Politik: „I'm a socialist. I was brought up in the communist coalfields of Fife. … I think our problems are problems of living in a monotheistic society and having a lack of gods and goddesses to which to refer.“ Zur schottischen Identität im Exil: „Debbie, my missus, is always staggered when we go back to Scotland and I just go back into the Scottish idiom. 'what are you doing? You've gone all funny'. 'I haven't gone fucking funny – I'm in Scotland!'“ Über die „broken men“ seiner Lieder: „Society shits the blame downwards and it's very interesting where the blame ends up and the people who hold the blame are really really interesting people in the way they deal with the blame. It's something that I find magnificent and eternal.“ Und über die „healing possibility“ seiner Songs: „people feel better for the pain the songs might give them“. [13]

Im letzten bekannten großen Interview (Ben Howarth, Pennyblackmusic, veröffentlicht 28. August 2011) beschrieb er die Arbeitsweise mit Michael Cosgrave an „Wayside Shrines and the Code of the Travelling Man“: „Basically what happens, I spend a lot of time in hotel rooms waiting for soundchecks, and I tend to come up with songs then. … He is like my editor.“ Die Platte sei „not a concept album. It's just a gathering idea of really good ideas that all developed in that way.“ Über Song-Entwicklung auf der Bühne: „you can't possibly understand why the bloody hell it is you wrote every single thing … I think anyone who pretends they do know all that stuff is just frankly lying.“ Über seine Bühnen-Geschichten: „I often think that I have bent the truth and I'm telling lies, and then to my astonishment I remember that what I am talking about did actually happen and is actually true.“ Zu seiner Zukunftsplanung, drei Monate vor seinem Tod: „Yeah, my job can be a hard job and I have to work hard to make a proper living, but I do make one. And I intend to carry on for at least another ten years.“ [15]

## Selbstbild: Versagen, Ruhm, Wanderer

Guardian 2001: „I am, an abject failure.“ — halb amüsiert notiert. Dazu: „I do feel ignored, but having courted it for so long, I can't feign surprise, shock, amazement and grief. I set out to see the world, not to be a rich and famous rock star. I'm comfortable down among the dead men …“ Und über die jährlichen Wanderungen mit Schlafsack: „I think aggressively: I have much latent aggression to the world, so I exorcise my violent fantasies to all and sundry, by walking down lanes … I come back nice and calm and it keeps me sane for the year.“ [3]

Chicago Reader 2010, auf die Frage, ob ihn die geringe Beachtung verrückt mache: „Yeah, sometimes it does. But then, I know so many musicians who complain that they're bored out of their minds with what they do … To which I say, 'What the fuck do you mean? Of course you've got a choice, you've just got to be willing to pay the price.'“ [14] Du Noyer erinnert an den Satz Levens zu einer Plattenfirma-Managerin, die ihm Gewichtsabnahme riet: „I'm a hero. … Heroes can be any size.“ [20]

Der Independent schließt mit dem Wanderer-Selbstbild: „My missus says, 'Jackie, you're a vagabond and I accept that'.“ [18] Das wohl letzte öffentliche Selbstzeugnis ist eine Online-Nachricht aus seinen letzten Monaten, überliefert von Du Noyer: „I have been a troubled soul most of my life although I have learned to be at peace with much of the troubles – a sort of Belfast Of The Mind in which the old conflicts remain raw in the imagination, but there is no real appetite for returning to the death ground.“ [20]

## Familie, Vater, Partnerschaft, letzte Jahre

Zum Verhältnis zu seinem Vater (gestorben im Juni 2000) sagte er 2001 über das Album „Creatures of Light & Darkness“: „After he retired, he had a love affair with Spain … We had a very complicated relationship, but it was made easier by him in the end. I carried on with a guitar and a sulk, always giving him my records to make him proud, but I was making no effort. He died feeling that his two sons and daughter were real people in the real world and he was happy about that. I'm fascinated by my way of grieving about my dad. The very least I could do was write a couple of decent songs about it.“ [3]

Über das Zusammenleben mit seiner Partnerin Deborah Greenwood: „She's explained it to me in 80/20 management terms: you can only do 80% of things and you must abandon 20%.“ [3] Greenwood selbst sagte nach seinem Tod über ihn: „He felt he was covered in 'electric fur' … He was prickly … He saw life very differently.“ Und: „He was a scary man when he was younger, and he could be scary when he was older as well. He was quite a presence.“ [20]

Aus Levens hinterlassenen Notizbüchern zitiert Du Noyer: „And my job is to listen / then I hear / then I write / then I sing / and I sing to those I heard / when I was listening / because those are your songs / not mine … I too have paid the price / And it was worth it“. [20] Zum Alkohol-Verzicht: Im Februar 2010, nach dem Tod seines jüngeren Bruders, habe er einfach aufgehört zu trinken — „just as he had with heroin, simply by deciding it was time“, noch bevor die eigene Krebsdiagnose (September 2011) kam. [20]

## Widersprüche und Unsicherheiten

- **Datum des Überfalls:** 1983 (Guardian 2001 [3], Guardian-Nachruf 1984 [17], Independent 1983 [18], Scotsman ohne Jahr [19], Herald 1984 [16], offizielle Biografie 1983 [6]). Die Mehrheit nennt 1983; Levens eigene Herald-Schilderung sagt 1984. Ungeklärt.
- **Tathergang:** „Slashed across the throat“ in der Market Street, Islington [3]; Erdrosselung von hinten an einem Geldautomaten in Islington [19]; Überfall auf dem Heimweg von Nord-London nach Maida Vale mit Strangulation [16]; „ribs broken“, Kehlkopf „almost destroyed“ [17]. Gemeinsamer Kern: lebensgefährliche Strangulation, monatelanger Verlust von Stimme/Sprache, dauerhafter Verlust des Falsetts.
- **Anzahl der Doll-by-Doll-Alben:** vier (Guardian-Nachruf [17], Kris Needs [1]) vs. fünf, davon eines unveröffentlicht (offizielle Biografie [6]) vs. „five eventful albums“ (Guardian 2001 [3]).
- **Gründungsjahr der Band:** 1975 (Independent [18], Herald [16]) vs. 1978 (Label-Text MIG [23]) vs. „formed in London in 1978“ (deutsche Wikipedia-Tradition). Leven selbst sprach 1978 davon, die Band sei gerade entstanden; Needs datiert die Zusammenkunft der Musiker auf 1974 (Dorset) mit Neuformierung später [1].
- **Albumtitel:** Der Independent-Nachruf schreibt „Gypsy Heart“ [18] — gemeint ist nach allen anderen Quellen „Gypsy Blood“.
- **Erstlingswerk „Control“:** 1971 (Biografie [6], Independent [18]) vs. 1973 (MIG-Labeltext [23]).
- **Herkunft der Eltern:** „beide Romany Gypsies“ (Independent [18]) vs. Vater Irish Cockney, Mutter Geordie mit Romany-Kontext (Biografie [6], Herald [16]) vs. „Sohn eines Iren und einer Roma“ (MIG [23]). Der Romany-Anteil väterlicherseits ist unsicher.
- **Geburtsname:** „Alan Moffatt“ nur bei Du Noyer [20] und einigen Sekundärquellen (Tumblr-Blog); von keiner Primärquelle mit direktem Leven-Zitat belegt — als wahrscheinlich, aber nicht verifiziert einzuordnen.
- **Bob-Dylan-Zuggeschichte:** Leven behauptete eine Begegnung mit Dylan in einer Berliner Hotelbar (5. Okt. 1988) und eine gemeinsame Zugfahrt Richtung St. Petersburg/Moskau, aus der die „Ko-Komposition“ „Moscow Train“ hervorging (erzählt im Dylan-Fanzine „The Telegraph“, Heft 51) [24]. Cooking-Vinyl-Chef Martin Goldschmidt bezeichnete diese Geschichte — wie die Whisky-Marke „Leven's Lament“ — als „complete scam“ [17]. Levens eigener Kommentar aus 2011 passt dazu: „I often think that I have bent the truth …“ [15] Als legendenhaftes Selbstzeugnis zu werten, nicht als gesicherte Tatsache.
- **Münchner/„Dalai-Lama-Bodyguard“-Geschichten** und ähnliche Anekdoten (u. a. das Weglaufen der Partnerin mit dem Leibwächter des Dalai Lama [3][4]) sind nur aus Levens eigener Erzählung belegt — typisch für seinen bewusst geschliffenen Geschichten-Stil („The stories sometimes take off in ways that are obviously fantasy“ [15]).
