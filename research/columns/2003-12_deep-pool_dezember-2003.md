THE DEEP POOL AND THE DARKEST NIGHT

I remember some years ago, my friend Jason Wright, who is now director of The
Core Trust, the holistic addiction centre which i co-founded some years ago,
and myself decided that we needed to exorcise the building from which the Trust
operated. Lots of old ladies had been dumped there to die before we took over
this whole row of cottages and turned them into one connected centre. Some of
the old ladies were pitiful in their final years and months, and we felt it
was right to try and address the lingering energetics of misery, and, indeed,
any other possible malevolent shapes that had gathered to wait over the years.

We decided that the thing to do was stay awake all night alone in the building
on the night of the Winter Solstice, and gathered material to read to each other
through the night, as it was important that we did not fall asleep. Jason was
better at this than i was, but there again, i had lived in these buildings before
they became Core, and was actually part of the source of psychic infection,
so part of me just wanted to sleep and continue the distress. Jason valiantly
read on, louder and louder, so that i could not depart the scene through fatigue
and mental scarring, and for this i shall always be grateful to him. The grey
dawn came, and we staggered out into the cold central London light, where the
poor were already gathering for their dismal battles with Social Security next
door in Tresco House, Lisson Grove, whilst halibut was being delivered to the
Seashell fish and chip restaurant which abutted the project at that time. We
walked past the closed second-hand bookshops where we had whiled away many a
happy hour finding obscure collections of poetry,and funny books you'd otherwise
not come across - 'Time Distortion In Hypnosis' springs to mind. Past the closed
Irish pubs and the Italian cafe where the man couldn't say egg and bacon, but
always said 'egg-a-bacon'. Past the Saturday flea market whose name i have just
forgotten, and past Paddington Green police station, where suspected IRA terrorists
were taken for interrogation. Somehow the locals always knew about the imminent
arrival of one of these suspected terrorists, perhaps because coppers with sub-machine
guns appeared in this part of the Edgeware Road about 2 hours before such arrivals.

I remember once my friend Scots John and me were leaning against the railing
watching this weirdly ancient scene of people gathering in anticipation of the
arrival of a particularily heavy Irish geezer. We had a bottle of codeine linctus
and a bottle of whisky to while away the hours, and absolutely nothing better
to do with our lives at this time, so we leaned and blethered, happy to be part
of the macabre pageant (is that how you spell it i wonder?). Suddenly a voice
said 'oi', and we turned round to see a sly grinning chip owner looking at us.
He said - 'so, are you two policemen then?' in a kind of jeering, mocking way.
John and me were quite good at ad-libbing in these sorts of situations, so i
took the lead. I got too close to this guy and said in a heavy east London accent
'So, is this your chip shop then mate?'

John came close as well, giving him a very hard scrutinizing look, and said
'ANSWER my colleague!'

The fellow completely bought it that we were indeed plainclothes policmen, and
stammered 'y y yyes'. I said well let's go inside, and i'll need to see some
proof of ownership. We all went in the chip shop which was full of the aforementioned
poor folk who had been at Social Security, eating chips with watered-down red
sauce, while waiting for the pubs to open so they could spend their giros in
a splurge. The guy said ' this is my wife - these men are policemen' he said
to her - 'tell them it's our cafe' he said nervously turning his hands inside
out (if you see what i mean). She anxiously confirmed this, so we looked at
each other, and i said we-ell, okay, but no more cheek, all right?', to the
bloke.

'No more cheek' he agreed nodding his head like Churchill the TV dog.

John said - 'also, we want some fish and chips', so they hurried to give us
a huge bag of stuff each - our first meal that day.

As we turned to go, two real plainclothes policemen sternly blocked our way.

'It's against the law to impersonate a police officer' this utter hardman said
- 'you're both under arrest'.

John started to pathetically explain that it was just a joke that had got out
of control.

'We watched the whole thing mate', the lead rozzer said, 'and you can hand back
those fucking chips for a start'.

This we did to hoots of derision from the assembled cast of low-life customers
who had been watching the whole scene with awe.

'C'mon' the copper said, indicating for us to leave the place with them - now
we were very unhappy - we knew the best that could happen would be that we would
spend a day and night in the cells, and then get yelled at in the morning. The
worst? - well....we're talking Paddington Green here, not a rural cop shop.

We trudged outside, heads down, trying to look completely hopeless and harmless,
while people on the top decks of buses pointed at us and frowned.

The lead filth put his face very close close to mine,looked at me for a long
30 seconds, then with no expression whatsoever on his face, said 'piss off'.

Our relief was like a tidal wave - we moved away down the pavement quickly,
John turned and said 'cheers mate' - the copper said go fuck yourself, and the
chip shop owner stood with his hands on his hips and a John Wayne-style look
on his face...

But what has this to do with me and Jason? Nothing really - it's just that i
can't remember what we did after we left the environs of The Core Trust, but
i do remember this other day i have described.

I'm about to go and get the train to play the 12-Bar Club in London, my last
show of the year, then i'm going home to light some Tibetan incense and have
a long think about my life and what i've become - the darkest night will soon
be upon us, and one must pay proper tribute to unknowable shapes - what is revealed
is always in a frame, and sometimes it's best to be like an infant, more interested
in touching the frame than looking at the contained image.

The songs keep flowing and i'll see you when the sun returns.

jl
